SKILL: cover a review topic subtopic by subtopic (evidence acquisition)
- A review is built from several distinct bodies of literature. Searching the topic as a whole finds the
  most-cited core and misses the rest.
- Before searching, split the question and its scope into 6-10 subtopics that together cover what a full
  review would discuss, for example: biology or mechanism; epidemiology and risk factors; diagnosis and
  biomarkers; each major treatment or intervention class; outcomes and prognosis; special populations;
  methods and models; open controversies. Each subtopic should match a section a human review would write,
  and subtopics should overlap as little as possible.
- Give each subtopic a short name and one clause saying which papers belong to it.
- Search each subtopic with varied query types:
    broad        2 queries naming the subtopic in general terms
    core-method  2 queries naming the specific methods, agents, assays or models it studies
    minimal      1 query of its 2-3 most essential words
    synonym      1 query using synonyms, abbreviations or older names for the same concepts
- Keep each query inside its subtopic but tied to the review topic, so the results stay relevant.
- Read the SUBTOPICS coverage counts: a subtopic whose queries now add few new papers is covered; a
  subtopic with few papers is where the next queries pay off most.
